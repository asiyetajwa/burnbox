The Idea

A calm box. Loud gear.

The brand comes from two references. The box is a quiet, machined stack of compartments: pastel, glass and chrome on studio grey. The gear is safety equipment that refuses to be dull: colour-blocked, stickered, a little cheeky. Everything inside Burn Box looks safe because it is. The colour is saved for what the sample tried to do.

Source One

The Box

  • Every detonation is one compartment in a larger cube.
  • Soft pastels, frosted glass, brushed chrome, white spheres.
  • Studio seamless grey, soft top light, long gentle shadows.
  • One hot pink dot is the only alarm on a calm surface.

Source Two

The Gear

  • Hard colour blocks that meet at clean edges, never gradients.
  • Stickers carry the jokes and the warnings: Super Safe, Canary, Burned.
  • Big condensed type wrapped across a surface, like a helmet name.
  • Small model codes in mono, like TRK H3, for runs and receipts.

Colour

Pastels hold it. Brights are the blast.

The containment set paints the product: surfaces, compartments, idle states. The blast set appears only where something happened: a canary grabbed, an egress blocked, a malicious verdict. Keep brights to stickers and single marks.

Rules

Keep it calm. Let the file be loud.

Do

  • Build screens on fog grey and paper, with compartments in containment pastels.
  • Save the blast colours for evidence: a canary, a blocked call, a verdict.
  • Name the product Burn Box, two words, both capitalised.
  • Write timestamps in UTC, in mono.
  • Use Title Case on buttons and links: Detonate It, Paste a Link, Download Receipt.

Don't

  • Use fire, skulls, hazard tape or hacker green. The box is a lab, not a horror film.
  • Put gradients on colour blocks, or glows on anything.
  • Write BurnBox, burnbox or BURN-BOX in copy. The repo slug burnbox is the only exception.
  • Fill empty space with stickers. One or two per screen, each marking a state.
  • Use em dashes or diagonal arrows on links.
